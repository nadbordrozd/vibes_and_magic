# Setting Checkpoint v2 — "The Mended Lands" (working title)

Canon for all content work. Supersedes v1. Every content prompt gets this file (and 07_MAGIC.md) pasted on top.

## The premise

**Author-side truth (never stated in game):** Long ago, many dying worlds were stitched together into one to save what could be saved. The stitching held. The memory of who did it, and why, did not.

**Player-side surface:** A warm, storybook medieval-fantasy land — meadows, castles, swamps, market towns. Centuries have healed the seams. But the land has oddities, every faction carries a fragment of a lost world without fully knowing it, and factions hold **contradictory** myths about the cataclysm (the Judgment, the Skyfall, the Great Mending, the Night the Bells Stopped). No game text ever adjudicates. Lore arrives via item descriptions, building flavor, rumours, and guardian tells — never lore dumps.

## Tone

**Warm, wondrous, wistful.** H2/H4 storybook surface; melancholy lives in the premise, not the palette. Never grimdark, never ironic. Flavor text: two sentences max.

## The Assimilation Laws

1. **Host grammar.** Medieval-ish fantasy is base reality; all factions accept its frame.
2. **Nothing arrives fresh.** Every foreign element is digested by ~500 years of weathering, misunderstanding, ritualization. No element knows its own origin accurately.
3. **Mundane first read.** Every anomaly reads as something familiar at first glance. The wonder is the double-take.
4. **Ration the strangeness.** One core anomaly per faction; one or two anomalous landmarks per map region.
5. **Nobody knows the truth.** In-world explanations are partial, ritual, or wrong.

## Visual identity laws

- Every faction owns a **material, a palette, and a silhouette language**; no sharing between factions. Any unit must be attributable to its faction from a small monochrome silhouette.
- **Two-dude rule:** max two human-shaped tiers per faction roster.
- Every faction must pass a seven-unit roster sketch with distinct silhouettes before confirmation. (All six below have passed.)

## The six playable factions

Format: name — origin (author-side) — innate verb — magic pair (see 07_MAGIC.md) — material/palette/silhouette — roster sketch (tiers flexible; 5 implemented now, 7 eventually).

1. **The Hearthguard.** Natives of the host world. Verb: **morale-meter manipulation** (banners, extra actions, rout resistance). Magic: **Rite + Craft**. Wool, wrought iron, heraldry; warm red/cream/gold; upright, shields, pennants. Anomaly: the relic banner's cloth is not from this world — and the kingdom's heraldic beasts step off it. Top tiers are **heraldry made flesh**: emblem-creatures that look like heraldic art, flat and bold-outlined. Sketch: Yeoman, Longbowman, Bannerman, Lance Knight, Hearth-Hound, Dexter Lion, Oriflamme Wyvern.
2. **The Unfinished.** The dead of the cataclysm — of every world — who died mid-purpose and keep trying to complete lives that no longer exist. Not evil; unresolved. Verb: **death triggers & recursion**. Magic: **Rite + Grave**. Bone, funeral linen, candlelight, grave goods; white/ash/candle-gold (never green-black); draped, hollow, each carrying an object. Sketch: Candle-Wisps, the Messenger, the Sentry, the Bride, Bone Choir, the Ferry (a funeral barge that crosses land), the Cortège (an entire procession as one unit).
3. **The Wound-Wrights.** A devout artisans' guild built around a fallen reliquary-hoard (author-side: a toybox). They build "saints' bodies," copying sacred forms whose purpose they've never guessed. Verb: **duplication & repair** (broken-not-dead). Magic: **Craft + Grave** (making in service of memory). Lacquered wood, tin, porcelain, stitched cloth; nursery primaries + brass; jointed, round-headed, seam lines. Sketch: Tin Soldier, Hobby Knight, Marionette, Jack-in-the-Box, Music Box, Stuffed Sentinel, Wooden Colossus. (Constructs are always *built*, never grown — see Hagwood.)
4. **The Vespiary.** A fragment of the **Broad World** — the world where everything was bigger. The insects aren't giant; they're normal-sized for home. Queens half-woke into speech at the merging and negotiate in formal, alien courtesy. Verb: **consumption & terrain** (casualties become biomass; resin/tunnel persistent tiles). Magic: **Craft + Wild** (living architecture). Chitin, amber, paper-nest; honey-gold/black/resin; segmented, asymmetric, papery wings. Sketch: Larval Tide, Paper-Wasp Lancer, Silk-Spinner, Amber-Carrier, Dragonfly Cavalry, Beetle Siege-Engine, the Half-Woken Queen. Map tie-in: carpet-forests and household-object landmarks are Broad World debris — max one such landmark per map.
5. **The Hagwood.** The stitched wildernesses didn't wake angry — they signed a contract. Every tree in that forest owes the Crones something. Verb: **bargains & curse-twisting** (hexes as tradeable objects; ownership as debt). Magic: **Wild + Grave**. Birch, wicker, bone-fence, crow-feather; birch-white/black/berry-red; crooked and asymmetric. Heroes are the Crones. Folkloric creature names permitted here (this faction mines the folklore vein). Everything is *grown or bargained*, never built. Sketch: Crow Chorus, Fence-Post Familiars, Besom Riders, Rusalka, Leshy, Wicker Giant, the Walking Hut.
6. **The Wildergrass Clans.** Steppe riders of a world that burned; they brought the herds and the ash. Verb: **blood price** — the meter inverse of Hearthguard: they gain momentum from their own losses. Magic: **Rite + Wild** (drum ceremony and herd). Hide, horn, ash; ochre/ash-grey/blood; low, fast, horned, plural herd-shapes. Sketch: Outrider, Drum-Caller, Ashmane Wolves, Aurochs Herd, Grass-Serpent, Thunderbird, the Stampede (the herd itself as one unit).

Canonical rivalries (opposed magic pairs, no shared school): Hearthguard↔Hagwood, Unfinished↔Vespiary, Wildergrass↔Wound-Wrights. Use these in map design and flavor.

## The hidden seventh faction

**The Seamborn.** Creatures of the stitching itself — native to no world, born where worlds grind together. No castle, no towns. They appear as guardians of essence springs, anomalies, and puzzle-locks, and are recruitable only at rare standalone dwellings in strange places. Encountering them quietly teaches the true shape of the setting; they are the lore-delivery mechanism. Design later; reserve the faction ID now.

## Canonical neutral cultures (former faction concepts, demoted)

The world has more cultures than castles. These exist as neutral guardians, dwellings, and map landmarks; either may be promoted to faction seven+ later:
- **The Gloaming Court** ("elves"): masked courtiers whom glamour made into what locals named them. Neutral dwellings and guardians: Glamour-Hounds, the Masked Duelist, the Nameless (wearing stolen faces).
- **The Order of the Unstruck Bell**: monks tending the place where time broke, keeping brass mechanisms they didn't make. Map landmark (monastery granting timing blessings) + the twelve **Hour-Beasts** as legendary neutral puzzle-lock guardians.

## Naming conventions

Homely-English-with-a-crack-in-it (Wound-Wrights, the Unstruck Bell). No apostrophe fantasy, no Latin/Greek constructions. Folkloric proper names allowed only for Hagwood content and the neutral pool. The cataclysm has no canonical name. Resources: Gold, Timber, Iron, Essence (essence pools where worlds ground together; essence-rich = anomaly-rich).
