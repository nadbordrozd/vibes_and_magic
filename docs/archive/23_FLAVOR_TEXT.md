## Hero stories

**Aldith** (Hearthguard) — Aldith was a fletcher's daughter who watched the levy break at Corsham Ford because the archers were placed like furniture. She reported this, loudly, to a knight, then to a captain, then to the old king, each of whom promoted her mostly to move the conversation along. Her longbow companies drill until the volley is a single sound. She still fletches her own arrows and considers this the only part of war that's honest.
**Corwin** (Hearthguard) — A hedge-priest who found that his sermons ran shorter and worked better shouted over a shield-wall. Corwin carries the liturgy in one saddlebag and the collection plate in the other, and has never lost a soldier who could still hear him. The relic banner once dipped as he passed. The church has opinions about this. Corwin, characteristically, has a louder one.
**Berta** (Hearthguard) — Berta ran supply wagons through the worst mud of three campaign seasons and holds the informal record for arriving before the message announcing her. She thinks in fodder, axle-grease, and hours of daylight. Knights bow to her now, having learned that glory travels at the speed of its breakfast. Her maps are annotated in a shorthand no scholar has cracked. It is mostly complaints.
**Osric** (Hearthguard) — The seventh son of a herald, Osric grew up grinding pigments for coats-of-arms and swears the old blazons are instructions, not decorations. He carries a different pennant for every occasion and reads battlefields the way clerks read ledgers. When his bannermen plant the cloth, soldiers report the strange conviction that the ground itself has taken their side. Osric just nods, as if the ground were on schedule.
**Petra** (Wound-Wrights) — Petra apprenticed as a mold-caster and fell in love with the arithmetic of many: many hands, many ranks, many small identical prayers marching in step. She knows every tin soldier's casting flaws by heart and calls them by lot number, affectionately. The Guild considers her promising. The soldiers, if asked, would consider her theirs.
**Silas** (Wound-Wrights) — Silas keeps the Guild's oldest ledgers, where the Founders' forms are sketched in a child's... in a *sacred* hand, he corrects, always. Grief brought him to the Guild — a workshop fire, long ago — and doctrine kept him. He speaks the withering words softly, like a man closing a door on a cold night, and his enemies find that things simply stop keeping.
**Grigor** (Wound-Wrights) — A tinker's orphan who survived a bad winter on what roadsides forget, Grigor sees the world as a floor after a feast: everything dropped, nothing truly lost. He walks slowly, arrives rich, and maintains that the Founders scattered the world's litter deliberately, as a lesson in looking down. The Guild indulges the heresy. His wagons keep arriving full.
**Mirele** (Wound-Wrights) — Mirele was a seamstress before the Guild found her, and the Guild found her because her mending held where armorers' rivets failed. She works the recovery-yards after every battle, singing the winding-songs, and her count of the returned is always a little higher than doctrine predicts. She refuses promotion out of the yards. Broken things know her voice now.
**Maren** (Unfinished) — Maren tends the smallest lights. In life — she does not dwell on it — she kept a lighthouse, and something, once, was not warned in time. Now she shepherds the wisps the way she wishes that night had gone: everything seen, everything reached, nothing going out alone. Her lantern is always lit. It is not clear what fuels it.
**Elgiva** (Unfinished) — Elgiva was a court scribe who died mid-sentence, and has spent the centuries since finishing other people's. She remembers names with terrible precision — the dead answer to her because being correctly named, after so long, is a mercy. Her writing hand never stops moving, even empty. Those she restores report a feeling of having been footnoted, kindly.
**Tobiah** (Unfinished) — The garrison at Tollhithe was ordered to hold the bridge until relieved. Relief, records suggest, was dispatched; the cataclysm intervened; the order stands. Tobiah has held the bridge, and every post since, with the patience of a man who no longer experiences boredom as separate from duty. He does not discuss the bridge. The watch continues.
**Brother Hollis** (Unfinished) — Hollis was a mendicant friar collecting for a chapel roof when the sky changed. The alms-bowl survives; the chapel does not; the collection, therefore, continues. He is gentle, apologetic, and absolutely unstoppable, and the offended dead gather to him because he alone still owes something and admits it. His enemies find that grief, organized, hits like a hammer.
**Vess** (Vespiary) — Vess was grown, not born, in the first hatching after the Falling, and the Queens speak through her the way wind speaks through a reed — constantly, and with corrections. She loves the larvae with an accountant's totality: each one counted, each one provided for, each one part of the sum. Outsiders find her warmth unsettling. It is entirely sincere. That is what unsettles.
**Oszra** (Vespiary) — Oszra oversees the renderies, and considers waste the only true blasphemy. Every fallen thing — friend, foe, unlucky bystander — is grief, and grief is protein, and protein is the future. She conducts funerals and harvests as one ceremony, with impeccable courtesy throughout. The neighboring villages have learned to decline her condolences early.
**Kettl** (Vespiary) — The lancers hatched restless, and Kettl hatched most restless of all. She has flown the borders of the Hive's memory and holds the record for formal apologies delivered mid-ambush. The Queens find her velocity theologically interesting. Her wing-cases are scarred in patterns the paper-wasps have begun, respectfully, to copy.
**Humm** (Vespiary) — Humm speaks rarely, which in a Broodspeaker is either failure or mastery, and the Queens have ruled it mastery. Where she walks, things grow — brood, blossom, resolve — as if her silence made room for it. She was present when the first Queen woke into words, and is said to be the only one who heard the sentence before the first one.
**Baba Zima** (Hagwood) — Zima is old the way winters are old: recurring. She keeps the wood's oldest contracts in a chest that audibly disapproves of visitors, and her bargains are famously fair, which is the frightening part. She has outlived every debtor who tried cleverness and attended each funeral with flowers. The flowers, too, were on terms.
**Yaga Olen** (Hagwood) — Olen tends the wood's grudges the way others tend bees: with gloves, smoke, and genuine affection. Blessings curdle in her vicinity — milk, luck, marriages — and she has made of this a precise instrument. Villages pay her to leave and invite her back within the year. She always knows which it will be.
**Old Marta** (Hagwood) — Marta raised crows before she raised the dead question of whose crows they were: the wood's, always the wood's, and Marta speaks for the wood. Her chorus knows every name in three valleys and uses them in sentences their owners regret. She seems kindly. The crows seemed kindly too, to the grain.
**Vasilisa** (Hagwood) — The youngest Crone by a century, Vasilisa came to the wood as a girl with a doll, an errand, and impossible tasks, and finished the tasks. The wood, impressed, kept her; the doll, notably, still advises. She rides ahead of her besom-wives laughing, and the laugh arrives places before the wind does.
**Temir** (Wildergrass) — Temir carried the drums at Ashfall, where the clans lost half the herds and learned the price could be spent forward. He wears a cord knotted once for every rider he has burned, and charges heaviest when the cord is freshly longer. The clans call him unlucky and follow him anyway. Grief, Temir says, is just fuel that mourns.
**Saiga** (Wildergrass) — Saiga ran with the ashmane packs as a child — the clans dispute whether she was lost or lent — and hunts now by their arithmetic: the bleeding first, the tiring first, the certain first. She speaks little and is understood immediately, which the wolves consider the whole point of language.
**Anai** (Wildergrass) — Anai was born during the year of long wind, and the clans say the wind never entirely left the tent. She reads the sky's moods like herd-sign and throws gales the way other riders throw ropes: casually, accurately, at things that were feeling secure. Her braids point windward. Always windward.
**Bataar** (Wildergrass) — Bataar has been ambushed eleven times and attended none of them. His outriders drill a single doctrine — be missed — and he grades them on the enemy's frustration, which he collects secondhand from prisoners with genuine scholarly interest. The steppe is large, Bataar says. Be more of it than they are.
